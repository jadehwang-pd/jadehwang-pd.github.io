Case study 01 · Maroo AI

Studio-quality portraits, no prompt required.

One selfie, three taps, a studio-quality portrait. This is how we got there, in four decisions.

RoleProduct Designer
TeamMe + 2 engineers
Timeline6 months · 2022–23
PlatformiOS · Android
WHAT I OWNED Everything except the engineering: user research, flows and UI, prompt and template design, pricing and refund rules, and per-region generation tuning.
ProblemAI could make the photo, but people had to fight prompts to get it.
Solution~1,000 templates with hidden prompts. Three taps, no typing.
ImpactAcquired at launch. 78% came back for a second photo.
ACT 1 · THE WALL So who was this really for?

The friends who gave up were our users.

Not AI power users. People who want a great photo of themselves, without learning photography or prompts.

GETTING BUY-INThat timer at the top wasn’t research. It was my argument.

My engineers wanted to keep the prompt box and just make it smarter. Instead of debating, I timed five friends until they got a photo they liked, then asked one question: would you use this again? The numbers ended the discussion, and we started designing for the people who had given up.

ONE WALL LEADS TO THE NEXTWhat every friend in the test ran into, in order
  1. 1›
    Learn a new language Keywords and negative prompts, before the first result.
  2. 2›
    Describe a look in words “Soft light, film grain, 85mm” is hard to say, even when you can picture it.
  3. 3›
    Retry until it works Rewrite, regenerate, repeat. ↺ back to step 2
  4. ✕ They gave up The average time to a good result was 30+ minutes. Too long to ever try twice.
WHY 2022 The tech and the demand arrived at the same time.
2× DALL·E 2 users doubled to 3M in a single month. Oct → Nov 2022 · DALL·E, Synthesia
60.8% of people in their 20s take studio photos every year. Embrain Trend Monitor, 2022
THE QUESTION I TOOK TO THE TEAM

How might people get the photo they pictured, without writing a prompt?

FORPeople who want pro-looking photos but find AI tools hard to use.
SUCCESS LOOKS LIKEA studio-quality result in a few taps, with nothing to type and no retries.
ACT 2 · DECISION 1 OF 4 Could a better prompt box fix it?

No. So we removed the prompt entirely.

Instead of teaching people to talk to the AI, each template does the talking. A pre-engineered prompt for light, lens and style hides behind every photo.

ACT 2 · DECISION 2 OF 4 Then how do you put their face into a template?

One photo and a face swap, not a personal model.

A personal model looked a bit more like you, but asked for 5–8 photos and up to a week. We do the hard part once, in the templates, so people only get the fast part.

WHAT I RULED OUT, AND WHY
Write better promptsToo hard. Even I had to study prompt guides online to get a decent result.
Train a personal model (LoRA)Better likeness, but too slow and costly for a first photo: 5–8 photos and up to a week per user.

The price of that choice: poses are fixed by each template. So the library had to be big, and I hand-tuned 1,700+ styles to keep every one of them good.

ACT 2 · DECISION 3 OF 4 What should people actually pay for?

Only photos that work.

AI fails sometimes, and paying for a broken face kills trust. Credits (blueberries) are spent only after a photo is made, and come back if the AI breaks the face. Taste isn’t refunded, so the preview and photo guide set expectations first.

ACT 2 · DECISION 4 OF 4 Then real users arrived. Did one setting fit everyone?

“Good” meant two different things.

After launch, reviews split by region. So instead of picking a side, I set different generation values per market: true likeness in the West, slight idealization in Asia. Same templates, same flow.

INTERLUDE · CRAFT How should an app that edits your face feel?

Soft and quiet, so the photos stay the hero.

An AI that edits your face can feel uncanny. Soft periwinkle, rounded shapes and a friendly mascot keep it playful and safe.

Acquired at launch, for what we built.

MAY 2023 · AT LAUNCH
Acquired The buyer saw a proven concept, clear demand and a template library ready to scale.
Dec 2022 kickoff → May 2023 launch & acquisition

The best AI experience is the one users don’t notice.

LESSONAbsorb complexity, don’t hand it overOur users had never written a prompt and didn’t want to learn. The right move wasn’t a better prompt editor. It was hiding the AI and letting them pick the photo.
LESSONShip, then listenWe couldn’t have predicted that “good” meant different things by market. Post-launch feedback, not assumptions, told us how to tune it.

Case study 02 · NAVER AI Tab

An AI that makes the call for you.

An AI agent inside AI Tab, the AI assistant of NAVER (Korea’s largest search engine). It phones a store, asks your question and reports back.

The hard part wasn’t the call. It was getting people to trust an AI with it.

RoleProduct Manager Intern
TeamMe + PM mentor
Timeline2 months · 2026
ToolsClaude · ChatGPT · Figma
WHAT I OWNED The whole feature, end to end: user research (two surveys and a 100-store field audit), flows, UX/UI, the clickable prototype, and the pitch through three executive reviews.
ProblemOutside Seoul, many stores only take bookings by phone, and people put off calling.
SolutionAn AI in AI Tab that calls the store for you. Watch it live, step in anytime.
Impact11 → 5 steps. Approved by a NAVER executive, patent in preparation.

01ProblemWhy build an AI that makes phone calls?

Booking often still ends in a phone call.

In Seoul, booking takes a few taps. Outside Seoul, many stores only list a phone number.

Not the phone. Who makes the call.

Case study 04 · Wonderful

A diary about your feelings should feel like one.

Wonderful is a self-esteem diary: log your mood, answer one question a day, take a small action for yourself. People liked the idea, but not the place they were writing it in.

RoleProduct designer
TeamPM · Frontend · Backend · Me
PeriodNov – Dec 2021
ScopeFull UI redesign
ProblemA feelings diary that looked like a work app. Only 14% were still writing after 3 months.
SolutionSame features, new tone: hand-drawn moods, one warm palette, a real-journal feel.
Impact3-month retention from 14% to 39%. Reviews started praising the design.
01The problem

People were writing about their feelings in something that looked like a dashboard.

Reviews and user feedback kept saying the same thing in different words: the app felt businesslike. Big saturated blocks, mood as text labels, every card shouting at the same volume. Nothing invited you to slow down and write. And people weren’t staying.

★★☆☆☆ · REVIEW“It feels like a work app, not a diary.”
SUPPORT“The design is too stiff. I don’t feel like writing here.”
★★★☆☆ · REVIEW“Love the idea. Wish it looked softer.”
Paraphrased from app reviews and user feedback.
3-MONTH RETENTION · BEFORE 14% Fewer than 1 in 7 users were still writing three months later.
02The insight

The features weren’t the problem. The tone was.

The daily question, the action and the story all worked. What didn’t work was how they were presented. A diary is private and emotional, so the redesign had one goal: keep every feature, and change how it feels to use them. The PM agreed to a UI-only release, so the developers didn’t have to touch the data model.

03What changed

Same screen, five decisions.

BEFORE
AFTER
01Mood
BEFOREWords in outlined circles: sad, happy, xoxo.
AFTERHand-drawn faces you recognize before you read them.
02Color
BEFOREFive saturated blocks competing for attention.
AFTEROne warm, muted palette. Brighter color only for moods.
03Texture
BEFOREFlat panels, like a settings screen.
AFTERA photo, a paper strip and tape labels, like a real journal.
04Hierarchy
BEFOREQuestion, Action and Story as three equally heavy cards.
AFTERToday’s question in focus; the three entries as soft, overlapping circles.
05History
BEFOREPast days as letters: S, H, X.

People started reviewing the design, not just the idea.

05Looking back

Tone is a feature.

In an emotional product, how a screen feels is part of what it does. If I did this today, I’d ship the redesign to a share of users first. Retention also moves for reasons outside the UI, and a split test would have shown exactly how much of the jump came from the design.

Case study 03 · Gongpal

What if you didn’t need a streak
— just someone to talk to?

A language app where you learn a new language by texting AI friends who remember you. It started with a problem I lived through.

RoleProduct designer (100%)
TeamMe + 1 developer
PeriodOct 2026
ToolsFigma · Claude Design · ChatGPT
ProblemStreaks keep learners going for days, not months. The hard part is staying motivated.
SolutionAI friends who text like real people and remember you. Learning happens inside the chat.
01Where it started

I moved to the US in middle school.
Every language app I tried, I quit.

Streaks kept me going a few extra days, never more. What I actually wanted was a friend who was there just for me — someone I could talk to whenever, in the language I was learning.

“Make a friend who only speaks English. You’ll pick it up fast.” — what everyone around me kept saying

They were half joking, but they were right. With someone you care about, you don’t think about lessons — you just need the language to reach them. Affection is the motivation.

That’s why almost every language app has a streak: the hard part isn’t the language, it’s staying motivated. So apps build external reasons to come back. I wanted to build an internal one.

EXTERNAL REASON“I have to study.”Streaks · daily goals · XP · reminders. Works for days, fades in weeks.
INTERNAL REASON“I want to talk to them.”A friend who remembers you and has a life of their own. The reason to return is the relationship.
02The signal

People are already hacking Character.AI to learn languages.

I wasn’t the only one. One user-made tutor bot alone has 33.5K chats. Learners want this — they just have to build it themselves, on a product that wasn’t made for it.

But it isn’t built for learning.
  • Setup is on you: no level, no corrections unless you prompt for them
  • Nothing is kept: no saved phrases, no review
  • Characters drift, so the relationship can feel unreliable

Language apps that add AI chat make the opposite mistake: it’s a tutor in disguise that steers you back to the lesson, grades every reply and ends when the exercise does. Learners had to pick: study, or feel connected. Gongpal is a friend, not a tutor.

03Design principle

It should never feel like AI.

If the relationship is the product, the friend has to feel real. So the whole interface removes every signal that says “this is a bot”: no AI badges, no sparkle icons, no instant answers. You should feel like you’re texting a person.

01Real faces
INSTEAD OF3D mascots, illustrated bots and “AI” badges.
GONGPALFriends look like real people: candid photos at work, on the street, in a café.
02Human timing
INSTEAD OFAnswers that appear the instant you hit send.
GONGPALThe friend reads, types, pauses, and often replies in two messages. It takes a few seconds, like a person.
03Real speech
INSTEAD OFTextbook sentences and perfect formality.
GONGPALCasual speech, slang and laughs, the way a native-speaking friend actually texts.
04Quiet learning
INSTEAD OFScores and red marks that stop the chat.
GONGPALOne line slips under your message after the friend replies. The chat never stops.
04First run

Three questions, then you’re texting a friend.

On Character.AI, setup is your job. Gongpal asks only what the friend needs to know, and every answer shows up as how they’ll text you, not as a settings form.

05Try it

Text Daniel. He takes a moment to reply.

This is the live prototype. Open a friend, send a message and watch a new language pick up through everyday chat.

Demo shown in Spanish.
06Trade-offs

Every call came back to one question: does this make the friend feel more real?

01No streaks, no XP, no leaderboards.

Every competitor uses them, and they do lift short-term return. But they build an external reason that fades, and they make the friend feel like a game character.

CHOSEPull users back with friends who text first and remember your day.
GAVE UPA proven short-term retention lever.
02Open conversation over scripted roleplay.

Scenarios are easy to build and measure. But a script reminds you it’s a lesson. Real-world practice still matters, so it lives in a separate Scenes mode.

CHOSEFree-flowing chats with friends; one-off Scenes (café, taxi) with goals and a review.
GAVE UPA tightly structured curriculum in the main chat.
07Iteration

Three things that made it feel like a bot.

Each early version worked, but somewhere it gave away that you were talking to a machine. So each one was redesigned around the principle.

01 · FIRST VERSIONReplies arrived the instant you hit send.Fast, but no person texts back in 0.3 seconds.
NOWRead receipt, typing, a pause, then two messages. A reply takes a few seconds.
02 · FIRST VERSIONCorrections came as a graded card.The chat turned into a quiz and stopped there.
NOWOne line under your own message, shown only after the friend replies.
03 · FIRST VERSIONFriends were illustrated avatars.They read as “an AI product” before the first message.
NOWReal-photo style friends with a camera-roll feel.
08The ending I’m designing for You come for the language.
You stay for the friend.
MVP coming soonMulti-language MVP in development
What I’m watching for
Do people open the app just to say hi?If they start the chat without a lesson in mind, the friend has become the reason to come back.
Do they keep phrases from the chat?A sign that learning still happens inside the friendship.
